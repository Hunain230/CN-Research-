The earlier remembered “about 82% Recall@20” could not be located as an aggregate Recall@20 result in the saved project outputs. Two saved **candidate-pair recall** results round to about 82%, and they measure a different stage from ranked incident recovery.

**Paper wording:** “A previous bounded candidate generator retained 81.25% of positive pairs in 68 sampled organization queues (54,052/66,527), but this was a retrieval-stage measure, not Recall@20. The best later exploratory predicted-group ranking result on the matched 57 small queues was 16.78% end-to-end Recall@20 under the 80/80 recovery rule.” State the exact cohort, denominator, and prior label exposure with each number. Do not compare these rows as if they were the same experiment.

The current record supports a reproducible **exploratory and limitations-focused** submission. It does not support a claim of 82% end-to-end Recall@20, model superiority, or operational SOC readiness. The previously accessed labels prevent a fresh confirmatory test; source membership and active-queue semantics remain unresolved; and the full-run 20-neighbor and 100-alert group caps impose mathematical limits documented in the [capacity audit](../outputs/submission_retrieval_bounds_20261114_v1.json). A new independently sealed labeled cohort is necessary to test a frozen improved method for a confirmatory performance claim. Review acceptance depends on the venue and reviewers and cannot be guaranteed by a reported metric.
