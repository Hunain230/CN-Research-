## Independent cohort required for a publication claim

The current GUIDE_Test queue rankings cover 499 organizations and 9,980 expert labels. Every organization or label set used in the current exploratory work has been exposed to method development. Re-running on the same files, changing the threshold, or choosing a favorable subset cannot create an independent test. A new labeled queue export must arrive with source provenance, a documented queue snapshot, an alert/evidence ledger, incident memberships, and expert top-20 rankings. The data provider must clarify rank direction, active-queue eligibility, and which evidence existed at alert creation. Before labels are opened, record input hashes, cohort eligibility, exclusions, method hashes, baselines, sample size, and the analysis plan. The [frozen contract](../outputs/future_cohort_study_contract_20261114_v3.json) specifies the current endpoint and metric definitions.

The [raw source-scope audit](../outputs/submission_absent_rank_scope_audit_20261114_v1.json) found that 855 of the 9,980 expert-ranked `(OrgId, IncidentId)` pairs are absent from GUIDE_Test incident pairs entirely. These labels remain in every reported denominator. A new export must demonstrate that ranked queues and candidate alert evidence refer to the same source snapshot.

The primary comparison uses the same alert scope for the new entity/time grouping method and the frozen rule plus transferred-ranker baseline. Report organization-macro end-to-end Recall@20 with all 20 expert labels per queue retained, the 80/80 recovery rule, paired organization intervals, candidate recall/coverage, pair precision/recall/F1, false merges, P@10, nDCG@20, high-priority misses, review load, and resource use. Do not tune candidate limits, missing-value rules, thresholds, grouping, or ranking after test labels are opened. A confirmatory superiority claim requires this independent cohort **and** a point-in-time field review; the current local sensitivity score cannot substitute for either.

## Field approval gate

The [Kaggle GUIDE data page](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/Microsoft/microsoft-security-incident-prediction/data) permits public benchmarking and encourages additional metadata use under its stated dataset license. The [publisher paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.09017v4) identifies `Timestamp` as alert creation time and documents anonymized entities and timestamp noise. Neither source states when each evidence row or entity value was attached to an alert in a live workflow. The local CSV has one alert timestamp repeated across its evidence rows, not a separate evidence-arrival timestamp. Therefore the [research-only entity/time policy](../configs/entity_time_sensitivity_policy.yaml) is distinct from the [empty operational allowlist](../configs/field_policy.yaml). To open the operational gate, obtain data-owner/SOC confirmation of field availability, processing scope, retention, and permitted deployment before using the fields in a live service or hosted model.

## Later shadow-mode SOC pilot

After field approval and independent offline evaluation, run a read-only shadow service against authorized live alert arrivals. It should emit proposed groups, priority order, evidence references, abstentions, and review flags **without changing incident records or triggering remediation**. Compare those proposals with analyst adjudication under a fixed rubric. Record alert arrival rate, p50/p95 alert-to-decision time, sustained throughput and backlog, failures/retries, analyst review load, false merges and splits, missed high-priority incidents, citation support, and resource use. Decide operating thresholds with the SOC owner before the pilot; no retrospective batch timing or fictional dashboard playback is a live-service measurement.

The pilot is blocked until an authorized feed, deployment environment, evidence timing/field approval, analyst review process, and retention rules exist. No live SOC access or new labeled cohort was supplied for this iteration.
