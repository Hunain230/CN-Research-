# Singleton and multi-alert ranker control

The [v8 code](../src/submission_specialist_ranker_v8.py) tested whether the descriptor-aware [v7 ranker](DESCRIPTOR_RANKER_ITERATION_20261114.md) should use separate priority models for single-alert and multi-alert groups. Both sparse logistic models used only the 334 historical training organizations and the same five descriptor fields and numeric features as v7. The 157,072 training groups included 149,124 singleton groups; 4,093 of 4,624 positive recovery targets were singletons. Candidates, predicted groups, evaluation labels, and denominators were unchanged.

The [frozen prediction record](../outputs/submission_specialist_ranker_v8_20261114_v1/prediction_freeze.json) shows that every specialist blend lowered development Recall@20 compared with v7 alone. The best eligible blend under the prespecified P@10 and nDCG@20 guards was **0.0**, so v8 copied v7 scores for all 499 queues. Development Recall@20 remained 0.4164; a 0.25 specialist blend reached 0.4134 and a full specialist score reached 0.4149. Development nDCG@20 was 0.3938 for v7, 0.3890 for the 0.25 blend, and 0.3839 for the full specialist score. The [separate evaluation](../outputs/submission_specialist_ranker_v8_20261114_v1/metrics.json) therefore reproduces v7's all-499 Recall@20 of **0.4982**, with no gain.

This is a negative exploratory result. V7 remains the best descriptive ranking candidate; v4 remains the previously registered future-cohort comparator. Neither v7 nor v8 changes the 0.6983 fixed-group oracle-order ceiling. Earlier access to GUIDE_Test labels, unresolved point-in-time feature availability, and lack of a new untouched cohort continue to prevent a confirmatory publication claim. The final report and PDF were not changed.
